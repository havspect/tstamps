# v2 sells one flat workspace subscription, and suspension is read-only

Status: draft, 2026-10-24. Amended by [ADR-0019](0019-workspaces-share-one-sqlite-file.md): the workspace state and billing mapping live in the `--db` database, not a host-level file beside the workspace files. Amends [ADR-0011](0011-host-workspaces-as-separate-sqlite-files.md), which left billing, suspension and deletion outside the design, and [ADR-0014](0014-self-service-workspace-signup.md), whose open signup now waits for payment.

v2 is a paid service the Operator runs. A workspace costs one flat price with unlimited users and starts with a 14-day trial. Stripe holds the subscription, invoices and payment methods; tstamps stores the billing contact, Stripe ids and the dates it needs to decide a state. When a trial ends or a payment stays failed, the workspace becomes **suspended**: everyone can still sign in, read the timeline, read reports, export CSV and pay; every write is refused. Data is never deleted automatically. Until Stripe ships, signup is operator-approved, so no free workspace exists without a decision.

Why flat: a per-workspace price has no seat counter, no proration, no plan checks in handlers and no feature gating, which is the smallest billing surface that can still take money. Why read-only: a customer locked out of their own timekeeping record will call the Operator, and export is the thing they must always be able to do. Why no auto-delete: v1 promises that nothing is hard-deleted (ADR-0004), and a retention job that erases a company's hours is the single most irreversible action in the product. Why a trial rather than a free tier: the product's value is visible in two weeks, and a free tier would need seat and storage limits that do not exist.

Considered and rejected: per-seat pricing (metering and proration for a team-sized product); feature tiers (SSO gated on billing state would make identity code fail during a payment hiccup); usage pricing (hours are not a cost driver and the bill would be unexplainable); a free beta (it contradicts selling, and free workspaces still cost support); full lockout on non-payment (hostage data); automatic deletion after 90 days (irreversible, and a policy nobody reads); manual invoices only (self-service signup would stay closed).

Consequences: a workspace state machine (`trial`, `active`, `past_due`, `suspended`) sits in a host-level database beside the workspace files, not in each SQLite file. Entering `suspended` stops running entries at that instant, because the read-only API could not stop them later. Stripe webhooks become a production dependency, and with them email. The billing contact is a workspace field, not necessarily a User, because finance addresses outlive people. Signup verification keeps its files but creates nothing until an Operator approves. `past_due` is a grace period, so "unpaid" does not immediately mean "cannot work". A suspension never destroys data, which means the Operator still owns the only deletion path and the privacy policy must say so.
